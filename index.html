Supplements: evidence guide

Supplements fill gaps; they cannot replace food, sleep or training.

Useful for many lifters

Creatine monohydrate

Strong evidence for improving repeated high-intensity exercise performance and supporting strength gains. A common adult maintenance dose is 3–5 g daily. Check with a clinician if you have kidney disease or relevant medical concerns.

Mass Gainer

1.Uses: Supplies extra calories, carbohydrates, and protein.
2. Best for: People who struggle to eat enough food to gain weight.
3. Note: Can add body fat if you consume more calories than you need. A homemade shake with milk, oats, banana, and peanut butter can be an alternative.

When needed

Vitamin D, B12 and iron

Useful when intake is inadequate or deficiency is identified. Iron should not be taken routinely without appropriate assessment.

Lower priority

BCAAs, fat burners and testosterone boosters

Often unnecessary when protein intake is sufficient. Fat burners and hormone-boosting claims deserve particular caution. Avoid products with undisclosed ingredients.

Usefull for meal prep

Whey protien,

1. Uses: Helps build and repair muscles after workouts.
2. Best for: People who struggle to get enough protein from food.
3. Types: Concentrate, isolate, hydrolyzed whey.
4.How to use: A serving commonly provides 20–30 g of protein, depending on the product.
5. Note: Whey isolate usually has less lactose than concentrate.

Pre Workout

1.Uses: May increase alertness, energy, and workout performance.
2.Common ingredients: Caffeine, citrulline, beta-alanine.
3.Note: Check the caffeine amount. High doses can cause anxiety, palpitations, and sleep problems. Avoid taking it close to bedtime.

Caffeine

1.Use: Can improve alertness and reduce perceived effort during exercise.
2.Best for: People who tolerate caffeine well and need a performance boost.
3.Note: Coffee is another option. Avoid pure caffeine powders, and don't combine multiple stimulant products without checking the total dose.

Beta-alanine

1.Use: May improve performance in repeated, intense exercise lasting roughly 1–4 minutes.
2.Typical studied dose: Around 3.2–6.4 g daily, often split into smaller servings.
3.Note:Tingling of the skin is a common side effect. It isn't a direct muscle-building supplement.

L-citrulline / citrulline malate

1.Use: May modestly improve blood flow and some aspects of exercise performance.
2.Best for: People experimenting with workout performance or training volume.
3.Note:Evidence is mixed; a muscle pump does not automatically mean more muscle growth.

How the body works

Muscle growth

Resistance training challenges muscles. Recovery and adequate nutrition help your body adapt over time.

Fat loss

A sustained energy deficit generally reduces body weight. Strength training and adequate protein help preserve muscle.

Hormones

Hormones regulate processes throughout the body. Sleep, adequate nutrition and overall health support normal function; supplements are not a shortcut to changing hormones safely.

Recovery

Sleep, rest, manageable training volume and consistent meals all support recovery and performance.
